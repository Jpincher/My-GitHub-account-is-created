Behind the experience

Start with the learner and the outcome

The intended outcome is practical: recognise warning signs and choose a safe way to verify a message. The activity gives learners a decision to make and feedback they can use immediately.

Adapt the support to the audience

The school example uses a familiar situation, short instructions and clear prompts. The university example adds an institutional context and asks learners to justify their judgement. Age is a starting assumption; prior knowledge, accessibility needs and learner feedback should guide further adaptation.

Use assessment to reveal understanding

A selected answer checks an initial decision. Asking learners to explain the warning signs and their verification steps provides stronger evidence that they can apply the learning. This short demonstration does not establish competence by itself.

Make the experience accessible

The page uses responsive layouts, labelled answer choices, keyboard-operable controls and feedback announced to screen readers. Instructions use plain language, and feedback does not rely on colour alone.

Evaluate and improve

In a live programme, I would review incorrect choices, learner explanations and feedback to identify confusing wording or missing support. A later activity with a different message would help check whether learners can transfer their understanding.
