How to read this site

This site is large and covers a lot of areas. You don’t have to read it from beginning to end. Think of this page as your map: choose what interests you, then follow a short route through the experience and the decisions behind it.

A small stick. Plenty to think about.

I built this project to make my learning-design judgement visible. The starting material is deliberately ordinary: a stick. The interesting part is what changes when the learners, their prior knowledge and the intended outcomes change.

School learners observe, predict, plan a fair test and explain what their evidence supports. First-year undergraduates work with quantitative models, uncertainty and the limits of a claim. You can explore the activities, teaching packs and tutor views, then inspect why I designed them that way.

For a recruiter, the aim is to offer something you can try and question: how I connect curriculum, activities and assessment; how I use an interface to support the task; and how I explain, test and revise a decision.

Finding your way

The left menu is your section map. Expand a heading to see its topics; the breadcrumbs and persistent header help you keep your bearings. Buttons beside explanations take you to the relevant page or section. You can move between the two learning pathways whenever a comparison is useful.

Use the language control in the project header for English, Spanish or Chinese. Hear guide voice starts the introduction for the current page; while it is playing, the button becomes Mute voice. The written introduction remains available, so you can choose the format that suits you.

What to look for as you explore

Look for the connection between an intended outcome, the task a learner actually completes and the evidence a tutor could assess. Notice how the level of support, the wording and the available controls change with the audience. Those small decisions are part of the instructional design.

What this example can show

This is a working portfolio study with fictional dashboard records and illustrative learning models. It makes the design and its reasoning available for inspection. Classroom effectiveness, practical delivery and accessibility still need evaluation with representative learners and tutors.

Follow your curiosity. You can always come back to this map.
